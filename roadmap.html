The Roadmap

From paycheck to wealth, in seven stages

It is not a rule. It is a map that answers the hardest money question: what do I do now?

Why a roadmap and not a rule

People who earn well are rarely short on advice. They are short on order. Does the bonus go to the mortgage or to investments? Is it time to ask for a raise or to change jobs? The roadmap exists so that answer takes a minute, not a month.

Two principles hold it up. First, income is the engine: nothing compounds until there is margin, and margin starts with what you earn. Second, ownership is the destination: the goal is not a bigger salary forever, it is owning assets that pay you whether you work or not.

The seven stages

1

Earn

Raise what you take home: pay range, job level, negotiation, benefits.

Income is the engine. Before you optimize investments, make sure what you earn reflects your level and the American market.

2

Control

Create margin between what comes in and what goes out.

Margin is where wealth starts. Know your cash flow, set a savings rate, and stop every raise from turning into lifestyle.

3

Protect

Emergency fund, insurance and expensive debt.

Before you build, make the base solid: cash for the unexpected, the right insurance, and a plan for high-interest debt.

4

Build

Invest systematically: tax-advantaged accounts and consistency.

Turn margin into capital. Use the right accounts, invest every month, and let your savings rate do the heavy lifting.

5

Own

Move beyond depending on a paycheck: stocks, real estate, a business.

Ownership is the destination. Assemble productive assets that pay you whether you work or not.

6

Multiply

Let compounding, taxes and diversification work for you.

Tune the machine: tax efficiency, diversification, costs and time. Small improvements become large differences.

7

Live

Financial independence: time, family, generosity.

Work becomes optional. Design the life money is meant to serve: time, people, generosity, legacy.

Three ground rules

  • You can be in two stages at once. Almost everyone is. Use the X-Ray to find the one that deserves your attention this quarter.
  • Earning power is a financial asset. A raise negotiated at 30 pays for the rest of your career. A career decision here is a money decision.
  • None of this is personal advice. The roadmap explains general principles. Your numbers, your taxes and your risks are yours; when it gets complex, work with a qualified professional.
